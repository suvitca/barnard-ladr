## Undergraduate research assistants

Most of our research assistants are Barnard and Columbia undergraduates. Research assistants work with video data from Nicaraguan Sign Language signers: coding signs, gestures, and facial expressions, checking each other's coding, and helping prepare analyses. Many also contribute to literature reviews, present at lab meetings, and go on to independent projects and summer research.

You don't need prior research experience or knowledge of a sign language. Helpful backgrounds include psychology, linguistics, cognitive science, anthropology, and Deaf studies. Familiarity with a sign language or with Spanish is a plus, but not required. Above all, we look for students who are careful, curious, and reliable.

Students usually commit to a regular number of hours each week for at least two semesters, which gives you time to learn our coding systems and contribute to a project.

## Independent research and summer opportunities

Advanced students can carry out a senior thesis or independent study in the lab. Barnard students can also apply to work in the lab through Barnard's Summer Research Institute.

## Graduate students and postdocs

If you are interested in graduate or postdoctoral work related to our research, email Dr. Senghas with a short description of your interests and your CV.

## Who are our alumni?

Former research assistants have gone on to doctoral programs in psychology, linguistics, and cognitive science, as well as to medicine, speech-language pathology, education, and technology. [Meet former lab members.](/people/#former)
